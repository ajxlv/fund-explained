**What this means.** The adjusted Rand index (ARI) is 1 when clusters match the official categories exactly and about 0 when they agree no better than chance. Official categories are SEBI rules about *what a fund may hold* (for example, at least 80% in the top 100 stocks for Large Cap). Clusters are about *how the price moved*. A low ARI means funds in different categories often behave alike, which is the finding, not a bug.

On demo data the categories were simulated with different factor loadings, so the ARI mostly reflects how the generator was set up.

## Limitations

- **Past behaviour only.** Every number describes history. None of it predicts future returns.
- **Survivorship bias.** Only funds alive today with at least 5 years of history are included. Funds that were closed or merged (often the poor performers) are missing, so averages look better than reality.
- **Direct plan, Growth option only.** Regular plans (higher fees) and IDCW options are excluded.
- **No expense ratios or holdings.** The label check sees prices, not portfolios, so a flag is a prompt to look at the portfolio, not a verdict.
- **One Nifty 50 index fund stands in for the Nifty 50.** Its small fees and tracking error are included.
- **Small classes.** With few funds per category, CV estimates are noisy. Categories under 8 funds are merged.
- **Synthetic data.** This whole report uses demo data. See the top of the page.
